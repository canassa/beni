# UI scaling sweeps, 2026-10-04

How each subject's cost per update grows as one parameter of the page grows. The owner's
question: beni's cost should grow with the size of the view and the data, Solid's with the
size of the change. Does Solid 1, or Solid 2, beat beni somewhere, and from what size?

Raw samples: [`2026-10-04-scaling-full.json`](2026-10-04-scaling-full.json) (every sample,
Chrome, Node and beni versions, the commit); a quick-mode run is
[`2026-10-04-scaling-quick.json`](2026-10-04-scaling-quick.json). Tables with interquartile
ranges: `node report-scaling.mjs results/2026-10-04-scaling-full.json`.

## Method

- **Harness**: `bench/ui/scaling.mjs`, the table benchmark's method (research 29 §1): one real
  click on `#go` in a fresh page, click to paint from a Chrome trace (`lib/trace.mjs`,
  js-framework-benchmark's analysis), after five warm-up clicks. Several samples per page,
  each in its own trace. Subjects in rotated order per page.
- **Programs**: generated per parameter value by `apps/scaling/*.mjs`, one per framework,
  each written as that framework's developer would write it: beni as TEA (`Tea.sandbox`,
  `view` in markup, keyed `For`, view helpers per level of a nested model); Solid 1 and 2
  with signals, `createStore` for a record of state and for a nested tree, `createMemo` for
  the derived list, js-framework-benchmark's signal-per-row for the table; P2 (research 29's
  hand-compiled TEA prototype); vanilla JS writing the one node. beni built at 2272c83bf9
  (`beni 0.1.0-m1`, ReleaseFast) in development and `--release`.
- **Throttling**: every point is measured at **4x** (js-framework-benchmark's rate for its
  update operations) and, interleaved on the same pages, at **1x**. The second was added
  because 4x is not usable below a millisecond: Chrome throttles by pausing the main thread
  in slices, and an update of 0.1 ms randomly absorbs a 0.4–0.7 ms pause — seen in the
  traces landing inside the handler, inside the microtask, and inside `DOMActivate`, which
  runs no JavaScript. So **the 1x column is the one to read for growth below about 1 ms**;
  4x is the harness's own number and is the one to read above it.
- **Samples**: 2 pages × 4 samples per throttle per point and subject (8 at 4x, 8 at 1x).
  Medians below; the JSON has every sample.
- **Machine**: AMD Ryzen 9 5950X, Chrome 153.0.8010.36 headless (nixpkgs `chromium`), Node
  24.19.0, Chrome pinned to CPUs 8–15 (`--taskset=8-15`). 1-minute load at batch starts:
  median 0.47, max 1.70. No failed samples. The full run took 3 014 s (50 min); the quick
  run, 334 s.

```sh
cd bench/ui && nix develop ../..#browser     # Chromium and Node; `node build.mjs` once before
node scaling.mjs --taskset=8-15              # quick: 2–3 points a sweep, 3 samples at 4x
node scaling.mjs --full --taskset=8-15       # full: every point, 8 samples at 4x and 8 at 1x
node report-scaling.mjs results/<date>-scaling-full.json
```

Cells are **script ms, median, `1x / 4x`**. "total" is click to paint at 4x, which includes
layout and paint.

## Caveats

- One headless Chrome, one engine, one machine, eight pinned cores; no other browser.
- **Paint-bound operations**: in the row sweeps above N ≈ 1 000, click to paint is layout and
  paint of the whole table and is equal across subjects within a few percent; the script
  differences above are real but small next to it.
- At 4x, any result under ≈ 1 ms is dominated by the throttler's pauses (above). The 4x tables
  are kept because they are the harness's method; their sub-millisecond rows do not order the
  subjects reliably.
- The burst sweep measures K synthetic `click()` dispatches; their cost is in every column.
- Development and `--release` beni are within noise of each other everywhere.
- beni-helper is one alternative of several for sweep 5; a program that keeps the projection
  in its model was not measured.
- Depth stops at 128 (beni's type-nesting limit, sweep 4); everything else stopped where the
  growth curves were unambiguous or where the page's layout made the click-to-paint time pass
  half a second (30 000 rows, 100 000 items).
