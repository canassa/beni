## 2026-10-31 11:28 CET — own2 F2: the cyclic join memo, specified

**What I did.** I specified F2 of #1372 in `ownership-v2.md` §2.9 (*The join and merge memos*, edited in place) and measured a prototype in a scratch copy. The prototype is not on any branch.

- **The unit is the position, not the state.** The `case` join of `Dict.removeMin`'s walk 2 walks 75 868 states at 29 624 positions. It has 205 distinct `(node, bits, run)`, but only 95 distinct sets of states at a position. A position holds 2.6 states in `removeMin` and 3 to 4 in the quadtree: a recast and its inners, or a union's sides. So a subwalk of one state shares its positions with its siblings' subwalks, and a memo per state is exact only where the state stays alone. A prototype per state, which validated aloneness and walked the call again when the check failed, saved nothing. It made the quadtree, T3 and `descend` 8 %, 2 % and 14 % worse.
- **The walk is position-major.** It walks a position's delivered states, then its children, then its folded states. Each position receives its states in the depth-first order, so a duplicate keeps its first arrival. The only difference, a folded state that removes a later delivered sibling, occurs on none of the 2 039 roots. The position-major walk alone changes no line.
- **The key** is the position's run of states plus a fold signature. The signature is each state's route above the position, restricted to the nodes that the subwalk looked up, as (state, node, height). A hit replays the external effects (fold marks, fold steps, folded states delivered to ancestors) at the same heights. At the end, the hit's position is the entry's local snapshot, or its bundle merged into the owner that collapses it.
- **Numbers.** `removeMin`'s join goes from 75 868 to 1 248 states, and from at least 1 071 819 to 139 768 records. The quadtree goes from 209 933 to 202 273, and T3 from 150 989 to 143 322. On #1459, `wa` goes from 295 179 to 267 917. `descend` and `incCs` do not change: their states are distinct. All 2 039 roots stay byte for byte the same, on master and on #1459, and the full cross-check panics on none.

**What I learned.** `removeMin` is still a limit, even with an allowance of 64 M. Once the early joins are cheap, the walk reaches later calls of 1 to 3.5 M records. Those calls have 300 to 700 distinct position runs and 17 to 36 states per position: distinct work that no memo removes. The other cliffs are not repetition either: on master, `wb`'s large calls have a few expensive states, and #1459 removes most of that cost (`wb` 2 461 162 → 216 222).
