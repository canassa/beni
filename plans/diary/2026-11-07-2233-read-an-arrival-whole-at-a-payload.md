## 2026-11-07 22:10 CET — read an arrival whole at a family's payload

**What I did.** Took over #2219 after the attack found `V1`, `Y2` and `G4Mutual`: refused on master, traps on the branch. The invariant was wrong. "A read at the payload is never empty" lets a read land at the wrong depth, so the rule is now that an arrival read at a caller slot at `X` is all of the arrival, at no suffix (`Subtree.wholeAt`, §2.1 *The exit rule*). `arrivalAt`, `feedAt`, `throughCall`, `ReadOff.callNodeAt` and `PlainReach` all take it. Two regressions came with it, and the sweep caught both: a run dropped a subtree symbol that may hold a function, and a row's narrowing dropped a `var` slot's depth, which a payload with no step is. Added the Depth fixtures with twins, a depth grid generator (`s4r/x/gen/d.mjs`), and audited every reader of a contract's tree.

**What I learned.** The first run of a whole read at 25 000 programs looked clean, and the same sweep's next 35 000 held 8 new traps: a fix's regressions show at the far end of the list, so a partial sweep says little. A family whose contexts are all functions has a payload with no step, and hash-consing makes that a plain `var`, so "has steps" does not say "is a payload". Most of what a family refuses on the truth list is not the graph: 436 of 572 are the summary-size limit on random families, and the oracle printed those as REFUSED until the coordinator asked. Half a day of numbers rested on a denominator that had dropped 887 programs; an oracle that does not share the checker is worth its setup. `--release` hangs on a function matched out of a family inside a callback (#2225), on master too, which only a fixture in `run/` showed.
