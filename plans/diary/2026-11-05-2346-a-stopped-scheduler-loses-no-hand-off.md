## 2026-11-05 23:46 CET — A stopped scheduler loses no hand-off (#1051 does not reproduce)

**What I did.** Checked #1051 on master `0426c9b155` with probe programs on two schedulers. The defect does not reproduce. A taker that the stop cannot interrupt (an `uninterruptible` region, or a release after its use returned) stays counted in `live`, so its scheduler stays in phase 2 past the deadline. The hand-off from the other scheduler goes on that scheduler's own queue, which still drains, and the taker runs with the values. The deadline cuts only a fiber inside a finaliser, and `abandon` runs its canceller. Added `run/QueueHandoffStoppedScheduler` and `run/SemaphoreHandoffStoppedScheduler`, four cases each. They are green on master. Three mutations of the emitted `_core/Task.mjs` make them red, and the third one gives the exact #1051 failure. Withdrew the claim in `transparent-effects-proposal.md` §17.9 point 7 with a dated amendment, and corrected the comment in `core/Queue.beni`. Opened #2093: a release that waits inside `withScheduler` of a second scheduler is not cut by the deadline of its own scheduler, because `expireS` selects by slot, not by membership.

**What I learned.** A claim worked out from the code needs two facts to hold, and here both were false: the dead end needs `live == 0`, which a parked taker prevents, and even the dead end leaves a masked or unwinding fiber its value, because `deliverable` needs `masks == 0`. When there is no fix to stash, a mutation of the emitted JavaScript is a quick way to show that a pinning fixture has teeth: copy `out/`, edit one kernel function, and run `node`. `beni build` takes `--core-root=<dir>` too, which `docs_test.zig` uses, for a mutation in the source.
