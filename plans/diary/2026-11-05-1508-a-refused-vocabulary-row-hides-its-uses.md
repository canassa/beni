## 2026-11-05 15:08 CET — A refused vocabulary row hides its uses

**What I did.** Worked on #1096 (PR #2014, a draft until the owner answers `vocab-refused-row-q1`). When the checker refused a vocabulary row, publication left the row out. A use of its name then fell through to a pattern, such as the wildcard `pub attribute "*" : String`, and the program got type mismatches before the platform's real error. I wrote the rule first, as an amendment to `checker-v2.md` §25.2. Now publication keeps the row, sets bit 30 of its fact word (`refused`) and gives it the scheme `<error>`. Resolution finds it by the usual precedence, and a tie that holds it answers it. A use is typed as an unknown name's is and reports nothing. The fixture `build/bad/VocabRefusedRow` holds six refused rows and their uses. Master gave 13 diagnostics, 7 of them misleading; the branch gives the 6 refusals only. Interface format 18 → 19.

**What I learned.** Two answers were possible: silence, or one diagnostic per use. `checker-v2.md` already chose silence for a failed value and a failed markup primitive (`<error>`), so I asked the owner and recommended silence. A tie matters too. Before, the earlier of two tied rows answered, so a use was checked against a row the platform did not mean. The vocabulary module's own view reads the failure bits when `Vocab.check` ends, and a bit set later (`sync_boundary`) refuses the row only at publication. That difference is harmless, because the module reports that error. A module that only uses a refused row now reports no error itself, so its markup section is written. That section writes the row as unresolved, so that no reader looks into it.
