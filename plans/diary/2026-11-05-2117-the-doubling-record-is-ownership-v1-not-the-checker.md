## 2026-11-05 21:17 CET — The doubling record is ownership v1, not the checker

**What I did.** #1238: `w_i = { a = w_(i-1), b = w_(i-1) }` checks in 197.6 s at depth 16. I timed it on master in eight forms, at depths 8 to 512, and split the time by phase with callgrind and `--self-profile`. Ownership v1 is the cost: 197.4 s of the 197.6 s, ×4 a level, and `check` runs it on every module, also with no platform. With annotations, the type checker and v2 stay far from exponential (polynomial, about ×3 when the depth doubles, #2074). Without annotations, they grow ×2 a level, with the type. v2 gives v1's verdicts with a write, at the floor where the type is linear. I added two `test-perf` scenarios, a pending one that stays red until v2 replaces v1, and a `check/good` fixture that pins both printed forms. Whether to keep Elm's let-polymorphism for `number` is the owner's question (q1).

**What I learned.** The issue's premise, "the source is linear, so a checker that shares structure stays polynomial", is false without annotations. `1` is a `number`, so each use of a let-bound `w` takes fresh variables, and the principal type of `w_d` has 2^d distinct ones: `dump --stage=types` is 575 KB at depth 13. No sharing can make it smaller. Measure each phase before you read a title: the issue said "checker", and 97% of the instructions were in `src/own/`. A ratio test at small sizes can hide a quadratic term under the process floor: 32 / 64 gave 2.14 with a floor of 16 M, and 3.3 without it.
