## 2026-10-18 14:54 CEST — test Protected in both directions in the falsifier

**What I did.** The review of PR #347 found that the link campaign did not test `Protected`: a `Protected` of every array or of none passed clean, because the generator named every view by its source. `Falsify.sample` now decides the naming after the live set is known. A view of a link component that nothing protects is named apart from its source, with the new origin added to the abstracts of the variables bound to it; that is legal, and the campaign must stay clean. A quarter of the views a live path protects are named apart too; `tryOne` expects the invariant to refuse exactly that (the `LinkNamed` message) and counts `linkMisnamed`, and it reports a failure when such a pre-state passes. Views an observer reaches keep their names, because an unescaped fresh origin would fail the observer clause first. New counters: `linkApart`, `linkMisnamed`, `postLinksWritten` (a post-state link whose view or source differs from the pre-state), `linkClause` (failures a link check reported). Two scratch mutants, `protectedArrays` as every array and as none, show both directions. `COVERAGE.md` gets the written counts and the chain's condition. The review's Low 3 and Note are issue #354.

**What I learned.** A generator that names states exactly as the invariant demands cannot test the predicate the demand depends on. To test `Protected`, generate legal states on both sides of it: a case the clause ignores and a case it must refuse, and assert each outcome. `sample`'s coin draws come after every existing draw, so the configuration stream of the earlier campaign is unchanged and its counts stay comparable.
