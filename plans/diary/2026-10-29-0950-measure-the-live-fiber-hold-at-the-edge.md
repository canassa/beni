## 2026-10-29 09:50 UTC — measure the live-fiber hold at the edge

**What I did.** The owner answered `cf-workers-q14` with A: renew the live-fiber timer every 14 minutes and measure it with one probe session. I had built A on a local branch (a 14-minute timer whose firing arms the next, a test hook that fires it early, a mutation proof), and ran the session: a plain-JavaScript Worker, `beni-probe-864`, on the account's `workers.dev`, with three Durable Object classes — no timer, the platform's hold (one 24-day `setTimeout`), and the 14-minute renewing timer — deleted after. After 16 minutes idle all three objects were new, and the renewing one had written no renewal. On the same deployment I narrowed it: the long timer kept its object for 60 s and not for 120 s, the renewing timer not for 8 minutes, the object with no timer not for 13 s. §9.12 (e.5) records it, dated; the code of A stays unpushed, and the owner has `cf-workers-q15`: a platform alarm every 60 s while a fiber lives, or the limit documented.

**What I learned.** A pending timer keeps a Durable Object from hibernating, the 10 s rule, but not from the eviction of an idle object, which Cloudflare's lifecycle page puts "after 70-140 seconds of inactivity (no incoming requests or events)". The 15 minutes the page also names are per operation, a different clock. The 15 s that #860 measured were true and still misled the design: a measurement at one time point says nothing about the next order of magnitude. A probe with one object per class can read each one only once per arm, since every read is an event that resets the idle clock. A design question that rests on a host fact goes to the owner with the fact measured, not assumed.
