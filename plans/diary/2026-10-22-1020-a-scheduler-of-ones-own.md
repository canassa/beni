**What I learned.** A scheduler cannot sweep its roots alone: a fiber spawned from a lent fiber belongs to the scheduler and is a child of a fiber of another, so the first `SchedulerStop` hung with nothing swept. A set of members is the fix. The release optimiser folds `if Js.read many` away, but what a hot path calls unconditionally stays: one `rootsFor` call outside the guard kept `schedulerOf` in every release program (+140 bytes) until guarded, and `closeAll` moved in place when a folded statement followed it, so the guard goes before the call it must not disturb. In development nothing folds, so a kernel path reachable from a hot path is paid by every fiber program; a closure in the scheduler's record makes the teardown reachable only from its maker and cut the development cost from 7.2 kB to 2.9 kB. `yieldNow` from the main fiber does not let fibers on another scheduler run first: the main fiber resumes in the same drain; a yield under the other scheduler does.
